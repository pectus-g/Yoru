## 1. Decisions I recommend (short version)

1. Evolve PR: not on retainer. Buy fixed-scope beats: an Announce beat (Dec 2026 or Jan 2027) and a Demo beat (May to July 2027, covering Next Fest and the Kickstarter if it happens). A Launch beat in early 2028 comes from a separate 2028 budget. You do community, social and dev logs yourself in every option.
2. Kickstarter: conditional go, decided in the week after Next Fest with hard gates (section 5). If gates hit: 30 days, launch 3 to 5 weeks after the fest, goal $35k to $45k CAD, digital rewards only. If gates miss: skip it and lose nothing.
3. Canada funding: apply to CMF Prototyping in the Oct 27 to Nov 10, 2026 window (consult must be booked at least 2 weeks before). Apply to the Ontario Creates IP Fund Pre-Production stream in the spring 2027 round. Put yourself on payroll now so OIDMTC pays out at launch.
4. Steam page public by end of Nov 2026, announcement trailer by mid Jan 2027 at the latest, so wishlists have 5 or more months to build before the fest.
5. Two social channels, 2 to 3 posts a week, every post built on one of the three hooks. No more channels than that; you are one person.

## 2. Positioning (what every asset says, in this order)

- One line: A two-tailed Nekomata judges the lost souls of a Japanese purgatory. Fight them, or talk them into peace. Every choice glows on her tails and reshapes the world.
- Hook 1, visible karma: rings on the tails, 29 world states. Show it, never explain it. The single best clip is a ring lighting up and the weather, fog and light changing in the same shot.
- Hook 2, full pacifist run: every boss can be persuaded instead of killed. This is the press hook. Comps that journalists reach for: Undertale (mercy), Ghost of Tsushima and Okami (tone), God of War 2018 (camera and combat).
- Hook 3, the dual form: a cat spirit who can become the grandmother she used to be. The Nekomata angle also puts you in front of the cat-game audience (Stray, Little Kitty Big City), which is large and starved for an action game.
- Always use the full title, YORU: Eclipse of Tails, in handles, page titles, tags and file names. "Yoru" alone is buried under a Valorant agent and a Chainsaw Man character in search. Hashtag: #EclipseOfTails.
- Before the announcement: run a trademark search on the full title (CIPO and USPTO). Cheap, and cheaper than renaming later. Not legal advice; ask a lawyer if the search shows anything close.
- Footage rule: nothing with placeholder animation goes public. The animator's deliveries gate the reveal, not the calendar.

## 4. Evolve PR: with, without, and the middle

What an agency gives you that is hard to replace: standing relationships with press and creators, pitch craft, embargo handling, meetings at shows, a second opinion on the trailer and page. What it cannot do: make people care about a game they cannot see. The demo and the assets carry the plan; PR amplifies them.

What you do yourself in every option: Steam page, social clips, Discord, mailing list, dev logs, showcase submissions, Kickstarter page. Evolve should not be paid for these.

| Option | Shape | Cost logic | Verdict |
|---|---|---|---|
| A. Retainer | Monthly from now to launch | 18 months of fees for 3 moments that matter | No. This is what got too expensive. |
| B. Fixed-scope beats | Announce (4 to 6 weeks), Demo (6 to 8 weeks), Launch (8 to 12 weeks, 2028) | Pay only around the moments press can act on | Yes. Ask for per-beat quotes, press and creators only. |
| C. DIY | Your press list, Terminals.io or Keymailer for distribution, own follow-ups | Cheapest; your time, weaker reach | Fallback for any beat whose quote is more than a third of the 12-month figure. |

Ask Evolve for, in writing: scope per beat, number of outlets and creators pitched, a results report, and whether they will do an Announce beat for a game with no demo. If they say the announce should wait for the demo, that is a fair answer and you go DIY plus Terminals for the announcement.

Note: Evolve is headquartered on Vancouver Island, BC, so their fees are not Ontario labour for OIDMTC. They may still count as marketing and distribution spend (the guideline does not say M&D vendors must be in Ontario). Ask Ontario Creates' tax credit team.

## 5. Kickstarter: can you survive there

The numbers: 443 funded video game campaigns in 2025 for just under $26M, 55 of them above $100k, 11 above $500k. Overall success rate on the platform is about 32%. The winning pattern in 2025 was a Next Fest demo first, then the campaign, with a mailing list in the low thousands before day one. Campaigns from Japanese companies fund at 58%; Japan-themed indies have a warm audience there.

Your realistic tier as a solo dev with a good demo and 8,000 to 15,000 wishlists: $30k to $60k CAD. $100k or more needs tens of thousands of wishlists, a demo that went viral, or a known name. I would not plan on it.

Gates (all three, checked the week after Next Fest):
- 8,000 or more total wishlists
- 1,500 or more mailing list plus Kickstarter pre-launch followers
- Discord with 100 or more people who actually talk

Goal math: goal = what your own list clears in 48 hours. Roughly 30% of pre-launch followers back on day one, at $60 to $80 average pledge. 1,700 followers x 30% x $70 = about $36k. Set the goal at or below that; stretch goals carry the rest. A goal you fund in two days gets Kickstarter's own promotion; a goal that limps for three weeks does not.

Costs: 5% Kickstarter fee, 3 to 5% payment processing, reward costs, about 1 month of prep and 1 month live with almost no dev time. Digital rewards only: name in credits, wallpapers, soundtrack, art book PDF, beta access, Discord role, a yokai or NPC named after the backer at the top tier. No physical goods; fulfilment kills solo campaigns.

What the money is for (pick one, name it on the page, make it visible): full animation for the boss roster, orchestral score and Japanese voice acting, or Japanese localization plus a console port. Backers fund a thing they can picture, not "development".

Why do it even with grants: it is market validation you can cite in the CMF and IP Fund production applications, a press beat, a bigger mailing list, and cash in hand you can show as the 10% contribution Ontario Creates wants from another source (the guideline does not name crowdfunding either way; ask the coordinator).

Why skip if the gates miss: a weak or failed campaign is public and follows you into press and grant conversations. Skipping costs nothing; the demo keeps collecting wishlists either way.

Canada specifics: Kickstarter supports Canadian creators and CAD campaigns. Funds are taxable income and rewards can carry HST; get the accountant to set it up before launch.

## 6. Canada funding path (stacked, in order)

| Program | What it funds | How much | When | Gate | Do now |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| CMF Prototyping (2026-27) | The demo, as a functioning prototype build, plus 10 to 25% of the budget on marketing and community | Lesser of 75% of eligible costs or $250k. Advance, repayable when the project moves into production | Window Oct 27 to Nov 10, 2026 | Canadian-controlled corp with head office in Canada; a shareholder with 1+ year professional experience; new applicants must do a pre-application consult at least 2 weeks before the close | Request the consult this week |
| Ontario Creates IP Fund, Pre-Production (Interactive) | Prototypes, demos, vertical slice, pitch materials, business and marketing plans | Up to $50k, 50% of eligible Ontario expenses, minimum request $15k | 2026 rounds closed April 13 and Sept 14; expect spring 2027; decisions within 16 weeks of the deadline | Ontario corp, 51%+ owned by Ontario residents; one owner or senior full-time employee with 3+ years full-time game or XR industry experience (school work excluded; nothing in the text says it must be Canadian); you own at least 51% of the copyright; 10% cash from another source, confirmed in writing | Email the coordinator to confirm your experience counts |
| Ontario Creates IP Fund, Production | The full game | $250k to $500k depending on track record, 50% of Ontario spend, minimum request $50k | Later rounds, 2027 | Same eligibility | Nothing yet |
| OIDMTC | Refund after completion | 40% of eligible Ontario labour (your T4 salary, Ontario contractors) plus up to $100k of marketing and distribution spend, for a non-specified product | Claim after the game is complete, within 18 months of that tax year end | 80/25 rule: 80% of dev labour to Ontario individuals, personal corps or sole props; 25% to your own employees. Wages, not dividends | Payroll and a labour log |
| SR&ED | Federal R&D credit | 35% refundable for CCPCs, on work with real technical uncertainty | Yearly with the T2 | Weak fit for most game work; the 29-state atmosphere resolver is the only candidate | Ask the accountant, do not count on it |
| CanExport SMEs | Travel and marketing abroad | Up to 50% of costs | Later | Needs revenue history; not yet | Revisit after launch |

Stacking: the IP Fund guideline sets no percentage cap on total public money, but Ontario Creates is a last-in funder: other financing (CMF included) must be confirmed before they commit, and a project under review at CMF is allowed. So the order is CMF first (decision Jan to Feb 2027), IP Fund second (spring 2027). IP Fund eligibility does not by itself make you OIDMTC eligible; OIDMTC is its own test. Ask the coordinator whether costs incurred before the deadline count (usually they do not).

Rights: the game stays 100% Pectus Games. Nothing above takes ownership. CMF gives a repayable advance (prototyping) or a recoupable share of revenue (production, 15% of gross until repaid or 7 years) and requires that you own and control the rights. The Ontario Creates IP Fund exists to fund Ontario-owned IP; its rule is that you hold at least 51% of the copyright, and you hold all of it. OIDMTC is a tax refund. Kickstarter backers get rewards, never equity or rights. No publisher. A porting or storefront contract is a licence to distribute, so read it for scope and term only, and walk away from anything that mentions ownership, sequels, or approval over creative decisions.
